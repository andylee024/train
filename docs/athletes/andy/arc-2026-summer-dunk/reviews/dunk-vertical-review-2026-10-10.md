# Dunk / Vertical Review: why the jump hasn't moved and whether to switch to THP

**Date:** 2026-10-10 (arc W20, Block 4 "405 Strength Push")
**Question:** I've trained consistently on a good split and got stronger, but my vertical is about the same. I can put a tennis ball on a 10' rim. The goal is to dunk a volleyball (I can palm one). Should I go back to THP-style training?
**Data:** Supabase `exercise_sets` / `workouts` / `daily_metrics` (Jan to Oct 2026), the arc plan files, the existing Block 1 and Block 3 reviews and the 3-month analysis. Research covered the THP ebook in `docs/content/training-styles/vertical-jump/sources/`, THP's own site and podcast, and the published research (sources at the bottom).

---

## 1. Verdict

**Yes, switch to a jump-first, THP-style block, and do it now instead of after the 405 squat.** Your vertical hasn't moved because jumping hasn't been trained since early July. There are three reasons:

- From Jul 6 to Sep 13 you logged **zero** jump or plyo sets. Since then the only jump work has been broad jumps and sprints done *after* upper-body lifting.
- The strength you've built is mostly **winning back** what you had in Jan–Feb. It isn't new strength beyond the level you jumped at in March.
- You're already at the squat-to-bodyweight ratio where adding more squat stops helping your jump.

On the evidence, the remaining gap (roughly 3–6") is realistic but not guaranteed over a 10–12 week, jump-first block. It would come from a combination of:

- **approach technique**, probably the biggest single lever for you
- **training jumps when you're fresh**, at full intent
- **modest fat loss**

- Every reactive exposure since September came at the *end* of a lifting session. That breaks the most basic VJ/THP rule: fastest work first, while fresh (VJ guide §4).
- Broad jumps and sprints are useful, but they are horizontal. They are not your test, which is a vertical approach jump to max touch.

### 2.3 Fatigue load: you've never jumped fresh

The v9 week is Sun heavy squat, Mon upper, Tue Oly, Thu Oly + front squat, Fri and Sat optional, plus BJJ four evenings a week.

- That's **two heavy squat exposures and four Olympic-lifting exposures a week**, on top of grappling.
- Saturday, the only day meant for jumping, was skipped almost every week (Block 3 review). It was optional, and by Saturday you're cooked.
- Research on squat sets taken close to failure shows that grinding work blunts jump gains even when it builds the same 1RM. Sets stopped early (about 20% velocity loss) improved the countermovement jump by ~9.5%; sets pushed further (about 40% velocity loss) improved it by only ~3.5% (Pareja-Blanco 2017).

### 2.4 Bodyweight and body fat: unknown since July

- Last logged bodyweight: **189 lb on Jul 30.** The profile estimates ~15–16% body fat against an 11% target.
- No bodyweight has been logged in the 10 weeks since, even though Block 4 calls the daily bodyweight log "non-negotiable".
- For a jumper, body fat is close to free height. As a rough physics estimate, losing **~10 lb of fat at the same strength is worth about 1.5–2.5"** (about 1" per 4–6 lb).

### 2.5 Measurement: the dunk goal has no data trail

- No standing reach, standing countermovement jump (CMJ), max approach touch or depth-jump test has been logged all arc. Every review since Block 1 has flagged this.
- "Tennis ball on the rim" is the only current data point.

## 3. How big is the gap?

This is an estimate. No published rule of thumb exists for volleyball dunks.

| Dunk | Approx. reach needed above the 10' rim |
|---|---|
| Rim touch (fingertips) | 0" |
| Tennis ball dunk | ~1–3" |
| **Volleyball dunk (palmed, 8.3" ball)** | **~4–6"** |
| Basketball 1-hand dunk (9.4" ball) | ~6" (common calculator default) |
| Basketball 2-hand | ~8" |

If a tennis ball on the rim means your max approach touch is about 10'1"–10'3", **you need roughly 3–6" more reach**. That's the same gap you had in March.

- **Realistic sources for it:**
  - approach technique: +1–3", per THP's view that the penultimate step is worth inches; individual results vary
  - fresh, high-intent jumping plus reactive work: +1–3" (stronger athletes who switched to ballistic work gained ~16% in Cormie 2010; meta-analyses of trained adults show ~1–2" on standing CMJ)
  - fat loss to ~180–183 lb: +1.5–2"
- **Stacked together, that's plausible in 10–14 weeks. It isn't guaranteed.** Vertical gains "come in spurts" (VJ guide §10).
- **Get a real number first:** standing reach plus max approach touch (§6), which turns the 3–6" estimate into an exact target.

## 4. Should it be THP, and why now rather than after the 405?

### 4.1 Why not finish the 405 squat first

Block 4 says to hand the dunk to THP once the squat reaches 395–405. By the block's own math that is a 2–4 month project, and the problem is that it's the wrong lever:

- **You're at ~1.9–2.0× bodyweight** (365/190). A study of 1,544 people found strength and jump height track together up to about 2.0× bodyweight, then flatten. Your own VJ guide says that past this point you should "pivot harder to RFD and reactivity".
- The VJ guide (§13) lists a "concurrent maximum-strength powerlifting peak" as **incompatible** with jump training.
- Cormie 2010 is the closest study to you: subjects squatting ~1.97× bodyweight gained more from **ballistic** jump training than weaker subjects gained from heavy lifting.
- Going for 405 first means the dunk sits idle for another 2–4 months, and at your strength level it's unlikely to add jump height.

**Recommendation:** park the 405 and keep the squat at maintenance (one heavy exposure a week). Come back to 405 after the dunk block, when the cost to the jump no longer matters.

### 4.2 What THP actually is (verified)

- **Who:** THP Strength (thpstrength.com), run by coach John Evans and pro dunker Isaiah Rivera (~50.5" approach vertical). Their podcast is active (Oct 2026).
- **Product:** individualized online coaching after an intake assessment. It includes video review of your technique, a group chat, "pain tracks" for knees and tendons, and deload weeks built in.
  - Prices aren't published. One 2024 review paid **$129/month** (programs delivered through the TeamBuildr app) and doubted how individualized it really was.
  - Their "30-day jump higher" guarantee **excludes anyone with more than 2 years of training age**, which includes you.
- **Philosophy, from their ebook (in your repo) and recent podcast episodes:**
  - **Technique is the #1 quality.** Evans makes the point that Olympic lifters with huge cleans often have approach verticals only in the high 30s because they lack the coordination pattern. Strength transfers only once the jump movement is learned. This describes you.
  - **Every jump at max intent.** Don't jump every day, jump 2–3 days a week, and stop when quality drops.
  - **Conjugate "high/low" structure:** all qualities trained all the time, load for 3 weeks then deload in week 4, and **cut the session before jump day** so you arrive fresh.
  - **They still lift:** "You should power clean. You should squat." Plus lots of short 10–20 m acceleration sprints, depth jumps for knee-friendly intensity control, and isometrics to fix weak positions.
  - **Approach:** groove a 3-step approach first and add speed only as fast as you can control it. Common faults are pushing early on the penultimate step and stutter steps.

### 4.3 Hire THP or run it yourself?

| | Hire THP coaching | Run it yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | ~$129/mo (2024 figure; confirm) | $0 |
| Best part | **Video feedback on your approach and penultimate step.** This is likely your biggest lever, and it's hard to self-coach | Fits your existing split, 6-exercise cap, Supabase logging and PR tracking |
| Risk | Generic programming; you must tell them about BJJ and Oly up front or they'll program around nothing | No outside eye on technique |
| Fit with your history | Simple weekly plan = good. A second external program stacked on DNT = **the Block 1 fall-off pattern** | Fine, as long as complexity stays capped |

**Recommendation:**
- If budget allows, **hire THP for 2–3 months specifically for approach coaching**, and pause or shrink the DNT Olympic program while you do. Don't run two external coaches at once.
- Otherwise, run the THP-style block below and film every jump session.

## 5. Proposed Block 5: THP-style dunk block (~10 weeks)

The following is a proposal. Nothing has been changed in the plan files.

### Principles

1. **Jumping first, fresh, at max intent, 2× a week.** Count only good reps, and stop the set or session when touch height drops ~1" or after two flat jumps in a row.
2. **Strength drops to maintenance.**
   - **Squat:** one heavy exposure a week (3×3 at ~80–85%), with 2–3 reps left in the tank.
   - **Power:** one ballistic lower session (jump squats, speed work).
   - **Front squat:** wave dropped.
   - **Power clean:** stays, as an explosive lift.
3. **No heavy lower or BJJ in the ~24h before the main jump day.** Heavy lower must sit ≥48h before it.
4. **Approach technique every jump session:** 3-step first, then a full approach. Film the penultimate step.
5. **3 weeks loading, 1 week deload and test.**
6. **The dunk becomes the PR you chase.** Max approach touch is logged every jump day, so the PR-tracking motivation now points at the right number.

### The week (≤6 exercises a session, one focus a day)

| Day | Session | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | **Jump A + Lower strength (HIGH)** | Approach jumps to a target: 8–12 max-intent reps, touch measured · Depth jump 18" 3×3 · Power clean 4×2 · Back squat 3×3 @ ~80–85% · RDL or hip thrust 3×6 · *(BJJ evening OK)* |
| **Tue** | Upper (LOW) | Bench wave · Weighted pull-up wave · Row/dips · mobility legs · *(BJJ evening OK)* |
| **Wed** | BJJ only | — |
| **Thu** | Speed + power (MED) | Sprints 5×10–20 m · Pogos/ankle hops 3×10 · Jump squat 4×3 @ ~75–95 lb · Bulgarian split squat 3×6 · Calves/tibialis · *(BJJ light or skip)* |
| **Fri** | Off or light upper | **No BJJ, no lower.** This is what makes Saturday work. |
| **Sat** | **Dunk day (HIGH, the priority)** | Warm-up → 3-step approach technique → 10–15 max approach jumps with touch logged → 5–10 volleyball dunk attempts → stop when it drops off |
| **Sun** | Off / side-split flexibility | — |

**What drops:** the front-squat wave, the Sunday heavy squat/deadlift day, and DNT down to **one** Oly exposure a week (the Monday clean). BJJ stays Mon–Thu.

If you'd rather keep the full DNT program, it has to come out of somewhere. It can't stack on top: the Block 1 lesson still applies.

### Phases

| Weeks | Focus | Jump work | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W0 (this week)** | Baseline test | Standing reach, best-of-5 standing CMJ, best-of-5 approach touch, depth jump from 18" | Bodyweight ×3 |
| **1–3** | Base and technique | Approach 8–10 reps, depth 18" 3×3, sprints 10–20 m | Squat 3×3 @ 80% |
| **4** | Deload + **test** | Half volume; re-test approach touch | Squat 2×3 |
| **5–7** | Intensify | Approach 10–15, depth from the best box height (test 18"→24") 4×3, add bounds | Squat 2–3×2 @ 85% |
| **8** | Deload + **test** | Re-test | — |
| **9–10** | Realize | Low volume, high quality, **dunk attempts every jump day** | Minimal (2×3) |

### Body composition

- Slow cut of **~0.5 lb a week** toward 182–184 lb. Protein stays high, and no aggressive deficit while jumping (VJ guide §9).
- Log bodyweight at least 3× a week. You can't credit or blame weight if it isn't measured.

### Guardrails, carried over

- **Patellar pain:** ≥3/10 means no depth jumps that week; ≥5 means a 2-week plyo deload.
- **"I fell off for a week":** resume at the current week.
- **6-exercise cap.**

## 6. Measurement (fix this before anything else)

| Metric | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Standing reach** | Flat-footed, one arm up, against the backboard or a wall with tape | Once |
| **Max approach touch** | Chalked or wet fingertips on the backboard or net, or a tape-measure strip; best of 5 | Every Saturday |
| **Standing CMJ** | MyJump-type app or wall reach; best of 3 | Weeks 0 / 4 / 8 |
| **Bodyweight** | Morning scale | ≥3× a week |
| **Dunk ladder** | Tennis ball → softball → volleyball → women's basketball | Log whichever you complete |

Log jumps in Supabase with the touch height in the set notes, or add a jump table, so the dashboard can show **max touch over time** next to squat e1RM. That pair is the explosive-strength-deficit tracker the VJ guide asks for.

---

## 7. Bottom line

- You did the hard part: 13+ weeks of consistency, plus real gains in bench and power clean.
- The vertical stayed flat for a simple reason: **it wasn't trained, and leg strength only returned to its winter level.**
- **Do this:**
  - Run a jump-first THP block now, with the squat at maintenance.
  - Get approach coaching, either from THP or by filming yourself.
  - Run a light cut.
  - Measure max touch every Saturday.
- **Expect:** if technique, freshness and weight come together, a volleyball dunk is a realistic 10–14 week target.
